> [!abstract] Key idea
> A **monopole** is half of a dipole, standing on a ground plane that acts as a mirror. It resonates at a **quarter wavelength** and radiates a **doughnut-shaped pattern**: equal all around, with nulls along the wire.

- The ground plane creates a **mirror image** of the monopole, so the pair behaves like a half-wave dipole.
- It resonates when its height is about **λ / 4**, which keeps it compact.
- Current flows up the wire, and the changing fields radiate outwards.
- Monopoles are simple, easy to build and used in mobile phones, Wi-Fi, LTE, RFID and IoT devices.

- It uses the **Wire** antenna template, simulated from 1 to 3 GHz.
- The ground is PEC (perfect electric conductor) and the monopole is a copper cylinder.
- **H = 31.23 mm** is a quarter wavelength at 2.4 GHz.
- The discrete port runs from the ground's face center to the monopole's circle center, across the small gap S.

> [!info] Why the port is 36.5 Ω
> A half-wave dipole has an input impedance of about 73 Ω. A monopole only has half of that structure, so its impedance is half: **36.5 Ω**. Setting the port to that value gives the deep dip. A real 50 Ω feed would need matching.

- Sweeping H shows that a **taller monopole resonates lower**. The notch also gets shallower.
- All the dips fit **f × H ≈ 72.5**, so 2.4 GHz needs **H ≈ 30.2 mm**.

> [!tip] Why it resonates low
> A thick wire acts electrically longer than it is. This one is 1.5 mm in radius, so it lands about 3% below the ideal λ / 4 prediction. Real monopoles are usually cut a little shorter than λ / 4.
