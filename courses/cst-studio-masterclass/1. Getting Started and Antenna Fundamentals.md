### 0.1.1 Project Templates
- A template **presets the units, solver and simulation settings** for one type of problem.
- Antennas belong to the **Microwaves & RF/Optical** environment.

> [!info] Why mm and GHz?
> Antenna size scales with wavelength. At 2.4 GHz, **λ = c/f = 125 mm**, so these units keep the numbers readable.

### 0.1.2 What CST Can Simulate
> [!grid]
> > [!info] Microwaves & RF/Optical
> > High-frequency waves: antennas, filters, periodic structures (such as FSS), RCS, optical devices and biomedical exposure.
>
> > [!tip] EDA/Electronics
> > Signal and power integrity in PCBs and packages.
>
> > [!warning] EMC/EMI
> > Unwanted emissions and interference between electronic systems.
>
> > [!example] Charged Particle Dynamics
> > Particle motion in EM fields, such as electron guns and accelerators.
>
> > [!note] Statics and Low Frequency
> > Slowly varying fields in motors, transformers and sensors.

> [!note]- Antenna types in detail
> - **Planar (patch):** a metal patch on a substrate above a ground plane. Thin, cheap and easy to make on a PCB.
> - **Waveguide (horn):** a flared waveguide with high gain and wide bandwidth.
> - **Wire:** simple conductors such as dipoles, monopoles and loops.
> - **Phased array:** many elements, with the beam **steered electronically** by changing each element's phase. A **unit cell** simulates one element to represent the whole array.
> - **Reflector:** a dish that focuses energy into a narrow, high-gain beam.
> - **Dielectric resonator:** radiates from a ceramic block instead of metal, giving low loss at high frequencies.
> - **Mobile and integrated:** compact antennas built into a device.
> - **RFID:** tag antennas for wireless identification.

- **Time Domain:** excites the structure with a short broadband pulse, so **one run covers the whole band**.
- **Frequency Domain:** solves at selected frequency points, one at a time.

> [!tip] Rule of thumb
> Time Domain is the usual starting point for antennas. Switch to Frequency Domain for narrowband, strongly resonant or electrically small designs.

## 0.2 CST vs HFSS
> [!grid]
> > [!info] CST
> > **Computer Simulation Technology.** Popular for antenna design because it is **accurate and fast**. Its Time Domain solver uses the **Finite Integration Technique (FIT)**.
>
> > [!tip] HFSS
> > **High Frequency Structure Simulator.** Uses the **Finite Element Method (FEM)**. CST's Frequency Domain solver uses FEM too.

## 0.3 RF Parameters
### 0.3.1 S-Parameters
- **S stands for scattering.** They show how RF energy travels between the ports of a network.
- At high frequencies, networks are described by **waves instead of voltage and current**.
- Z, Y and H parameters need short and open circuit tests, which aren't practical at RF.
- A 1-port system, such as a single antenna, only has **S11**. A 2-port system has all four.

> [!tip] Design target
> **S11 < -10 dB** at the operating frequency, so about 90% of the power enters the antenna. At **-3 dB**, half is reflected.

> [!warning] Accepted is not the same as radiated

### 0.3.3 Why S11 Should Be Below -10 dB
- Power that isn't reflected enters the antenna: **P<sub>t</sub> = P<sub>in</sub> (1 - |Γ|²)**.
- Γ converts to dB with **S11 = 20 log|Γ|**.

### 0.3.4 S22 Parameter
- **Output-port reflection coefficient.** Works like S11, but at port 2.
- Together, S11 and S22 give **return loss, impedance, admittance and VSWR** for both ports.

### 0.3.5 S21 and S12 Parameters
- **S21 (forward transmission):** power from port 1 delivered to port 2. Also called forward voltage gain.
- **S12 (reverse transmission):** power from port 2 delivered back to port 1. Also called reverse voltage gain. Useful for evaluating reverse coupling.
- Both give **gain or loss, insertion loss, phase, group velocity and group delay**.
- Used in **EBG** (electromagnetic band gap) and **FSS** (frequency selective surface) structures to see how much signal passes through or is absorbed.

### 0.3.7 VSWR
- **Voltage Standing Wave Ratio** = Vmax / Vmin along the feed line.
- Measures the **mismatch between the antenna and its feed line**. Reflections create standing waves.
- Range: **1 (perfect match) to infinity**.
- **VSWR < 2** is acceptable for most antennas. Above 2 means a poor match at that frequency.
