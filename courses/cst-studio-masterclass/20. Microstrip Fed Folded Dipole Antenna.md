> [!abstract] Key idea
> A **microstrip line can feed a printed dipole directly**: the strip drives one arm and the ground drives the other. Bending both arms fits a half wavelength onto a small board. In this design, the **second arm** is what finally brings the match below -10 dB.

- The substrate is **FR-4**, 1.6 mm thick, with εr edited from 4.3 to 4.2.
- The **front** has a feed line that continues into a bent arm.
- The **back** has a short, truncated ground under the feed line, and a second bent arm growing out of that ground.
- The 3 mm strip works out to about **52 Ω** on this board, which is close to the 50 Ω target.

> [!warning] Not a classic folded dipole
> A classic folded dipole is a **closed loop** of two parallel conductors, which raises its impedance about four times, to around 290 Ω. The arm ends here are **open**, so "folded" just means bent to save space. There's no impedance step-up.

- Following the current, it flows out along the front arm and back along the back arm. On the two horizontal bars, that's the **same direction**, so their fields add.

> [!info] Still a half-wave dipole
> Measured along the copper from tip to tip, the path is about **83.5 mm**. A half wavelength at 1.65 GHz is 90.8 mm in free space. So this is a **half-wave dipole, bent to fit**.

- **One arm:** with only the front arm, this is really a monopole, and S11 stays around -5.5 dB.
- **Ground shifted:** reducing D from 11.5 to 5 mm helps only a little.
- **Second arm:** S11 reaches -13.4 dB at 1.65 GHz, and stays below -10 dB from about **1.59 to 1.70 GHz**, a bandwidth of 6.7%.
- The far field is a dipole's near-omnidirectional doughnut, and the surface current runs from the port along both arms.

> [!tip] The ground shift also makes room
> A centered ground ends at x = 13 mm, but the second arm starts at 13.5 mm, so it would miss the ground. Shifting the ground puts it **under that arm**, so the two connect.

> [!info] A poor match isn't zero signal
> At -5.5 dB the antenna still accepts **72%** of the power. -10 dB is the usual target, where 90% is accepted.

## 0.4 Sweeping the Board Width
| Substrate width | S11 at the dip | Power reflected |
|---|---|---|
| 70 mm | -13.9 dB | 4.1% |
| 80 mm | -13.4 dB | 4.6% |
| 90 mm | -12 dB | 6.3% |

- A narrower board matches slightly better, and a wider one slightly worse.
- The copper doesn't change here, so this is only a small loading effect.

> [!warning] Read how much, not just which way
> The lecture calls 70 mm "a lot lower" than 80 mm, but that's only **0.5 dB**, or about half a percent of the power. Only the dip depth was read, so any frequency shift isn't known.

- The same layout with new values moves the dip from 1.65 GHz to **0.89 GHz**, at **-35.67 dB**. The lecture's plot shows S11 below -10 dB from about **0.80 to 0.92 GHz**, a bandwidth of about 14%.
- The simulation runs from 0 to 1.2 GHz.
- The tip-to-tip path is about **189.5 mm**, against a half wavelength of 168.6 mm, so it's still a bent half-wave dipole.
- The copper sits in the lower half of the board, so the board could probably shrink. A sweep like the one above would show by how much.

| Dimension (mm) | 1.65 GHz design | 0.89 GHz design |
|---|---|---|
| Board, SW × SL | 80 × 30 | 160 × 120 |
| Feed length, F | 8 | 25 |
| Ground, W × L | 26 × 8 | 70 × 25 |
| Ground offset, D | 5 | 13.5 |
| Arm spacing, S | 12 | 21 |
| Front arm, L1, L2, L3 | 11, 10.75, 7 | 30, 29, 18.7 |
| Back arm, L4, L5, L6 | 11, 10.75, 12 | 29.8, 24, 22 |

- The strip stays **3 mm** wide, because the substrate is the same and the line must stay near 50 Ω.

> [!tip] Reuse the model
> With every dimension as a parameter, you can usually save a copy and enter new values instead of redrawing. Check the result with **E**, because the two lecture models measure L2 and L3 slightly differently.

> [!info] Not a plain scale-up
> The path grew 2.27 times, but the frequency only fell 1.86 times. Plain scaling would predict 0.73 GHz, not 0.89 GHz. The one-step correction from [[19. Dipole Antenna|Section 19]] suits a straight dipole. For a bent one, expect to sweep as well.

> [!warning] GSM isn't one 800 to 900 MHz band
> The real bands are **GSM 850 at 824 to 894 MHz**, and extended **GSM 900 at 880 to 960 MHz**. With a band of about 0.80 to 0.92 GHz, this antenna covers **GSM 850** and only the lower part of GSM 900. It misses the 925 to 960 MHz downlink.

> [!warning] Check the monitor frequency
> The far field monitors here were at **0.6 and 1.2 GHz**, not at the 0.89 GHz dip. So the doughnut shown isn't the pattern at the working frequency. Add a monitor at the dip before judging it.
