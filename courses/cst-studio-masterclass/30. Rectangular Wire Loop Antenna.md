> [!abstract] Key idea
> Despite the course's title for this section, this is a plain wire loop made of four cylinders, with **no capacitive loading**. It's still a useful check: its dip lands right where the one-wavelength rule predicts. It also shows why a pattern must be read at the working frequency.

- Four PEC cylinders, each 15 mm long and 1 mm in diameter, form the loop, using the Wire template.
- Two cylinders stand upright, and the other two are a rotated cylinder and its translated copy.
- The plain layout leaves small notches at the corners, so the upright cylinders move in by 0.5 mm before the four parts are joined with Boolean Add.
- A brick cuts a 2 mm gap in the bottom cylinder for a 50 Ω discrete port.
- The lecture states no target frequency.

> [!warning] No capacitive loading here
> All three lecture titles mention capacitive loading, but the slides and the model have **no capacitor**. The only gap is the port gap, so treat this as a plain wire loop.

- A capacitor across a gap in the loop cancels the loop's inductance, so a loop much **smaller** than a wavelength can resonate.
- In CST, it would be a **lumped element** across a second gap.
- The price is a narrower bandwidth, as with most ways of shrinking an antenna.

> [!warning] Read at the wrong frequency
> The narration says 2 to 6 GHz, but the plots run from 0 to 10 GHz, with far field monitors at 5 and 10 GHz. So the pattern comes from **5 GHz**, where S11 is only about -3.9 dB and 59% of the power gets through. At the 5.65 GHz dip, 86% is accepted. Add a monitor at the dip before judging the pattern.

> [!info] The same two claims
> The lecture again calls the pattern a doughnut and says the loop suits only short-range RFID. As in [[27. Rectangular Loop Antenna|Section 27]], a one-wavelength loop has its peak along the axis, and a mismatch of this size doesn't limit it to short range.

## 0.4 The Perimeter Rule
| Loop | Mean perimeter | Dip | Perimeter / λ | 325 / perimeter |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Square strip, Section 27 | 112 mm | 3.06 GHz | 1.14 | 2.90 GHz |
| Ring, Section 28 | 103.2 mm | 3.15 GHz | 1.08 | 3.15 GHz |
| Wire loop, this section | 58 mm | 5.65 GHz | 1.09 | 5.60 GHz |

- For a first guess, **f in GHz ≈ 325 / mean perimeter in mm**, which is a perimeter of about 1.08 λ.
- It lands within 1% for the ring and this wire loop, and within about 5% for the square strip.
- It's a rule fitted to these models, so use it as a starting point and then tune with a sweep.
