> [!abstract] Key idea
> This section improves a compact monopole **one change at a time**: ground stubs, then etched slots, then patch stubs. Comparing each step shows what every change really does, which is a good way to work on any design.

- The stepped shape keeps the antenna compact: the whole board is only 16 × 18 mm.

> [!tip] Build on what you have
> Each design only adds to the last one, and **Mirror Copy** makes the second stub or slot. Save each step as its own file, so you can compare them later.

## 0.3 Results
| Design | Deepest S11 | How far it stays below -10 dB |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | About -26 dB | Rises to about -9 dB within the band |
| 2 | -26.5 dB | Up to about 9.87 GHz |
| 3 | -29 dB | Up to about 9.74 GHz |
| 4 | -32 dB | Flatter, about -11 to -12 dB across the band |

- The far field is doughnut shaped at 4 and 9 GHz, tilting slightly at 9 GHz.
- At 14 GHz the antenna works poorly, because S11 has risen above -10 dB.

> [!warning] More stubs aren't automatically better
> The lecture concludes that adding more stubs always helps. The numbers show each step **deepens the notch**, but the upper band edge barely moves, from 9.87 to 9.74 GHz. Design 4's real gain is a flatter curve inside the band.

> [!warning] The stated band isn't shown
> The lecture quotes a band up to 13.35 GHz, but none of the results shown stay below -10 dB that far, and the upper edge is around 10 GHz. Treat 13.35 GHz as unconfirmed.

> [!info] What to compare
> Judge each change on bandwidth, how flat S11 is across the band, and the far field. As in [[17. UWB Monopole Antenna|Section 17]], the deepest notch alone tells you very little.
