> [!abstract] Key idea
> Shaped arms and a small stub give one printed dipole **two bands**, one for each WLAN range. The useful lesson is how each part tunes those bands, and why a result must be checked against the **real band limits**: this design misses its own 5.2 GHz target.

- It targets the **2.4 and 5.2 GHz WLAN** bands, and is simulated from 0 to 8 GHz.
- The substrate is FR-4, with εr edited from 4.3 to 4.4. The 3 mm feed line works out to about **51 Ω**.
- Each face carries one dipole arm, with the substrate between them. The back is the **mirror image** of the front.
- Each arm has a stem, a horizontal arm, a wide block at the tip, and a small stub near the base.

- The two stems sit directly on top of each other, so they form a **parallel-strip line**.
- A microstrip is unbalanced, with a narrow strip over a wide ground. Parallel strips are **balanced**, which suits a dipole better.
- This differs from [[20. Microstrip Fed Folded Dipole Antenna|Section 20]], where the two arms sat side by side.
- At 16.6 mm, the stem is about a quarter wavelength at 2.47 GHz, so it probably helps the match too.

> [!tip] One parameter or two?
> **Share a parameter** when parts must stay equal: both faces use the same W1, L1 and W2, so the arms stay mirrored. **Use separate parameters** when values only happen to match: D and L4 are both 2.5, and FL and GL are both 22, but each can then be swept alone.

| D (mm) | Upper dip |

- Moving the stub changes **only the upper band**. The lower dip stays where it is.

- The deeper dip sits near 6 GHz, so it doesn't help the 5.2 GHz target.
