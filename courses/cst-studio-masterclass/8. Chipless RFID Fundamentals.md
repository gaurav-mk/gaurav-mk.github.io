> [!abstract] Key idea
> **RFID** identifies objects by radio, with no line of sight. A **chipless RFID tag** has no chip: its metal shapes store the ID as **notches in the reflected spectrum**, one notch per bit.

- A **tag** normally holds a microchip (IC) and an antenna. The chip's memory stores a unique ID.
- The **reader** emits radio waves, which carry both energy and communication.
- The **backend** processes the data and links to systems like inventory management.
- Unlike barcodes, RFID needs **no line of sight**, works automatically and can read many tags at once.

## 0.2 Tag Types
| Tag | Power | Range | Cost | Bands |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Active** | Own battery | Up to 100 m | High | UHF, microwave |
| **Passive** | Reader's signal | Up to 10 m | Low | LF, HF, UHF |
| **Semi-passive** | Battery runs the chip, reader's signal carries the reply | Medium | Moderate | UHF |
| **Chipless** | None, the geometry stores the data | Short to medium | Very low | UHF, microwave |

> [!warning] Semi-passive tags
> The lecture says these use the reader's energy for the chip and a battery to transmit. It's normally the other way round, as shown in the table.

## 0.4 How Chipless Tags Work
- Chipped tags can be costly, aren't always disposable, and can be unreliable on metal or wet surfaces.
- A chipless tag is **cheap and fully printable**, which suits tagging individual items, smart packaging, documents, and humidity or temperature sensing.
- The reader sends a **wideband** signal. Each resonator on the tag reflects less at its own frequency, which leaves a **notch**.
- **A notch is a 1, and a missing notch is a 0.** Notches need enough spacing so they don't overlap.

- Each set has **seven hexagonal rings**, so this is a **7-bit tag**.
- Rings are cylinders with **Segments = 6**, as in [[2. Antenna Shape Design|Section 2]]. For a hexagon, the radius equals the side length.
- The substrate is **FR-4 (lossy)**, 1.6 mm thick, on a copper ground plane of the same size.
- The second set is a **Mirror Copy**. Having two sets side by side gives deeper notches.
- A 0.3 mm radial width measures 0.26 mm across the hexagon's flats, which is expected.

> [!info] Why smaller rings give higher notches
> A ring resonates when its perimeter is about one wavelength in the substrate. That puts ring 1 near 5.5 GHz and ring 7 near 19 GHz, which fits the 4 to 20 GHz band.

## 0.6 RCS Simulation

> [!warning] This doesn't give 14 bits
> The lecture says two polarizations double the tag to 14 bits. But the same symmetric rings respond at the same frequencies in both, so both carry the **same 7 bits**. The real benefit is that the tag reads correctly at any orientation. Independent bits need resonators that respond to only one polarization, as in [[10. Dual Polarized RFID Tag|Section 10]].
