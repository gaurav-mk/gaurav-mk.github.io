> [!abstract] Key idea
> A laptop antenna doesn't work alone: the **metal frame of the screen is part of it**. This printed loop is meant to be grounded to that frame, with a U-shaped element tuning seven bands. The model shows how much the frame matters, but its loop never touches the frame, and the low GSM bands don't appear.

- The board is FR-4, with εr edited from 4.3 to 4.4, at 65 × 10 × 0.8 mm. It's simulated from 0.5 to 4 GHz.
- **Front:** a rectangular loop with a middle strip, opened by a 1 mm gap in its bottom side. One end of the gap runs to the feed strip, and the other runs down to the board edge.
- **Back:** a U-shaped element, with its base on the board edge and its arms running up behind the loop.
- The target is **hepta-band** laptop use, which means seven bands: GSM 850, GSM 900, DCS, PCS, UMTS, WLAN and WiMAX.

> [!warning] H must be 4 mm
> The table lists H = 6 mm, but the strips then need 12 mm on a 10 mm board. The lecture changes H to **4 mm**, which fits exactly.

- The loop is fed at one end of the gap, and its other end is meant to be shorted to the frame.
- That makes a path of about 137 mm, about a half wavelength near **0.9 to 1.1 GHz**, depending on how much the FR-4 slows the wave. That's the low mode for GSM.
- Higher modes of the loop, and the middle strip, would then cover the upper bands.
- The U element's arms sit 0.8 mm behind the loop's strips, so they couple to it. The slide says this adjusts the resonant modes to cover the bands.

- The frame is a 200 × 160 mm copper sheet on the back layer, with the antenna on its top edge, 3 mm from the corner.
- The U element's base touches the frame, so the U element is grounded.
- The frame is large for this antenna: 1.5 × 1.2 λ at 2.25 GHz.

> [!warning] The loop never touches the frame
> CST's own readout puts the frame at **Z = 0**, and the strip that should reach ground is on the front, at **Z = 0.8 mm**, with no via. So the loop isn't shorted as the slide intends, and only the U element connects. That could be why the low band is missing, though the lecture doesn't test it. To match the slide, join the strip to the frame with a via or a copper bridge across the board edge, then simulate again.

> [!note] Check the port too
> The waveguide port sits on the end of the feed strip, which has no ground plane under it. The real antenna would be fed across the gap, so a discrete port from the feed strip to the grounded strip is the closer model.

- Adding the frame moves everything: the dips, the depth and the efficiency.
- The two bands have bandwidths of about 11% and 9%.

> [!tip] Simulate the antenna where it will live
> Radiation efficiency jumps from **26% to 86%** just by adding the frame. An antenna that's meant to use a chassis as its ground can't be judged alone, so include the chassis from the start.
