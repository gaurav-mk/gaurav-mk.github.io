> [!abstract] Key idea
> A microstrip patch antenna is three layers: a copper **patch**, a dielectric **substrate** and a copper **ground plane**. A **feed line** carries the signal to the patch, and the patch size sets the frequency.

- Feed and patch are joined with **Boolean Add**, so they act as one conductor.

## 0.3 Discrete Port
- A **port** is where the signal enters the simulation. CST offers waveguide ports, discrete ports and plane waves.
- A **discrete port** is the simplest. It runs from the ground plane up to the feed, so its length is the substrate thickness.
- Its impedance here is **50 Ω**.

## 0.4 S11 Results
- One port means only **S11** is available.
- Simulate wide first (1 to 10 GHz) to find the dip, then narrow (1 to 3 GHz) to see it clearly.
- This design's dip falls between 2.4 and 2.5 GHz, below -10 dB.
- **Markers at -10 dB** read the band edges: 2.392 to 2.493 GHz, so about **100 MHz** of bandwidth.

## 0.5 Field Monitors and Gain
- **Field monitors** tell the solver which field results to save, and at which frequency. Add them before simulating.
- A **far field** monitor is what gives gain and the radiation pattern. Others save E-field, H-field, surface current, current density and power loss density.
- At 2.4 GHz: **gain 2.94 dB, realized gain 2.59 dB**, with the main lobe at 0° along +Z.

> [!info] Why the gain is low
> FR-4 is a lossy substrate, and these dimensions were picked for teaching, not performance.

- **Surface current** flows from the feed across the patch. Uneven current suggests where slots could help.
- VSWR, Z matrix, Y matrix and balance results are also available.
- Far field results can be viewed in 3D, Cartesian or polar plots.
- Fields can be animated, and current can be shown as arrows, streamlines or bubbles.

## 0.6 Parametric Sweep
- A **parametric sweep** simulates a range of values for a variable, to show what it does instead of guessing.
- Number of runs = range / step + 1. PW from 35 to 37 in 0.5 steps gives 5 runs.
- Open it from Simulation -> Par. Sweep, then add each parameter and its range.

![[Pasted image 20260821122819.png]]

| Variable | Swept (mm) | Effect on S11 |
|---|---|---|
| Patch length PL | 27 to 29 | **Shifts the frequency** |
| Patch width PW | 35 to 37 | Changes dip depth and bandwidth |
| Feed width FW | 1.5 to 3 | Makes the dip wider or sharper |

> [!info] Why length sets the frequency
> The patch resonates at about **half a wavelength** in the substrate, so a longer patch resonates lower.

> [!tip] Sweep coarse, then fine
> PL from 27 to 29 mm in 0.5 mm steps found the region. PL from 28 to 28.5 mm in 0.1 mm steps found **28.3 mm**, which puts the dip on 2.4 GHz. Then fix that value and simulate once more.

| Design | Patch length | -10 dB band | Realized gain |
|---|---|---|---|
| Before tuning | 28 mm | 2.392 to 2.493 GHz | 2.59 dB |
| **After tuning** | **28.3 mm** | **2.377 to 2.435 GHz** | **2.55 dB** |

> [!warning] Keep thickness fixed
> Substrate and copper thickness come from the board material, so treat them as fixed and tune the other variables.
