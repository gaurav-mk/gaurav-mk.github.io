> [!abstract] Key idea
> Two tall **semi-elliptical petals** make a wide folded dipole, driven through a **slot** by a microstrip line on the back. The feed is a classic wideband design, but the lecture's model leaves out a short piece of the slot, so its results **don't show this antenna working as drawn**.

- The substrate is **Rogers RO4003C**, 1.52 mm thick, simulated from 2 to 24 GHz. The lecture doesn't state a target band.
- Each petal is a semi-ellipse with a smaller one hollowed out at its base, so it stands on **two feet**.
- Smooth, curved edges are known to give wide bandwidth, as with the [[17. UWB Monopole Antenna|elliptical patch in Section 17]].
- Both petals join the ground, so current has a **closed path** around them. That makes this a truly folded shape, unlike the open arms of [[20. Microstrip Fed Folded Dipole Antenna|Section 20]].
- The petals and ground are on the front, and the feed line is on the back. The 4.1 mm line works out to about 46 Ω, a little below the slide's 50 Ω.

> [!info] Two values for RO4003C
> CST's library gives εr = 3.55, which is edited to 3.38. Both are real: Rogers lists **3.38** as the process value and **3.55** as the value recommended for design.

- This is a **microstrip-to-slot transition**: the line crosses a slot in the ground at a right angle, and the signal moves from the line into the slot.
- For that to work, the line must look like a **short** at the crossing, and the slot must look like an **open** beyond it.
- The radial stub and the circular end do this over a wide band, where simple quarter-wave stubs would be narrowband.
- The slot then leads up into the 0.3 mm gap, which feeds the two petals. The slide says this gap is tuned for the 50 Ω match.

> [!warning] The model is missing part of the slot
> On the slide, the gap runs **through the ground into the circle**. In the lecture's model, the gap stops at the ground's top edge, and the circle is a separate hole 3.3 mm below it. So the feed crosses **solid copper**, with no slot to excite. To match the slide, subtract a 0.3 mm wide slot from the ground, from the gap down to the circle.

> [!warning] This isn't dipole behavior
> At 18 GHz the board is about **5.4 × 7.2 wavelengths**. The lecture calls the pattern almost omnidirectional, but it's lumpy with many lobes, and 7.32 dBi is far from a dipole's 2.15 dBi. The surface current is strongest around the feed and weak on the petals. An 80 mm petal is a quarter wavelength near **0.94 GHz**, so 15 to 20 GHz isn't its natural band. All of this fits the missing slot, so treat this band as a result of this model, not of the antenna on the slide.

> [!tip] When a result looks odd, plot the current
> **Surface current** shows which parts are really working. If the part that should radiate carries little current, the feed isn't reaching it.
