> [!abstract] Key idea
> This compact printed Yagi makes one strip do two jobs: the strip on the back is both the **feed return** and the **reflector**, and a via links it to one dipole arm. The result is a small, wideband antenna that covers 2.4 GHz WLAN and shows a real forward beam.

- It's printed on FR-4, 1.6 mm thick, on a board of only 38 × 66 mm, and simulated from 1 to 4 GHz.
- From left to right, the front has a **director** strip, a **dipole**, and the **feed** strip.
- Tip to tip, the dipole is 58.8 mm. The back strip reaches 64 mm, so the reflector stays slightly longer, as a Yagi needs.
- The 28 mm director is short, which keeps the antenna compact.

- The feed is two 4 mm strips stacked on the two faces, which works out to about **52 Ω**. It's a balanced line, like the stems in [[21. Dual Band Dipole Antenna|Section 21]].
- The top strip runs straight into the lower dipole arm.
- A **via** carries the back strip through the board to the upper dipole arm, so each strip drives one arm.
- The 0.8 mm gap between the two horizontal strips is a short slot line leading to the dipole.
- The back strip carries on past the via, and that extension is the **reflector**. It can also be used to adjust the bandwidth.

> [!tip] Modeling a via
> Subtract a cylinder from the substrate to drill the hole, then add a copper cylinder of the same size to fill it. Check that it touches the copper on **both faces**.

- The main beam points **towards the director**, along -X, so this one shows real Yagi behavior.
- Several resonances overlap to make the wide band. The dipole is about half a wavelength near 1.86 GHz on this substrate, so the first dip is probably its own.
