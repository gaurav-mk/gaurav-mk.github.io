> [!abstract] Key idea
> A **dipole** is two metal arms fed at the center, about **half a wavelength** long. It's the antenna many others come from, such as monopoles and Yagi arrays. Because resonance depends on length, one simple scaling step can tune it.

## 0.1 What a Dipole Is
- It has two metallic arms separated by a small gap, with a total length of about **λ / 2**.
- Hertz and Marconi used dipole-type antennas in the earliest radio experiments.
- It's the base of **monopoles, Yagi-Uda arrays and log-periodic antennas**.
- Its predictable behavior makes it a **reference antenna** for calibration and measurements.

- Fed at the center, the dipole resonates. Current is highest at the feed and zero at the open ends, and voltage does the opposite.
- The alternating current creates changing fields that radiate.
- Radiation is strongest **perpendicular to the arms** and weakest along them. That gives the same **doughnut pattern** as a [[15. Monopole Antenna|monopole]], which is a figure of eight in a 2D cut.

> [!info] 73 Ω or 50 Ω?
> In free space, a half-wave dipole naturally has about **73 Ω**. Printed dipoles are tuned to **50 Ω**, so they match standard cables and RF circuits without extra parts.

## 0.4 Uses
- FM and TV broadcasting, Wi-Fi routers, Bluetooth, RFID and mobile base stations.
- EMC testing, as a reference antenna.
- Satellite links, where **crossed dipoles** create circular polarization.

- It uses the **Wire** template, simulated from 1 to 3 GHz, with L = 62.46 mm, which is a half wavelength at 2.4 GHz.
- The rod is a copper cylinder along Z. A second cylinder of **Vacuum** forms the feed gap.
- **Boolean Insert** places the vacuum inside the rod, which splits it into two arms.
- Pick the two gap edges, then add a **discrete port** set to 73 Ω, because there's no ground plane and this is a free-space dipole.

- Since f × L stays almost constant, scale the length by **measured frequency / target frequency**.
- Enter the correction C as a parameter, then multiply L by it. One step moved the dip to **2.388 GHz**.
- A second pass with C = 0.995 gives about 55.16 mm, which should land on 2.40 GHz.

> [!tip] Faster than a sweep
> When frequency depends mainly on one length, this scaling lands very close in a single run. Use it first, then fine-tune with a sweep if needed.

> [!info] Why it started so low
> This rod is thick, with a length only about 21 times its diameter, and thick antennas act electrically longer. That's the same effect as the monopole in Section 15, but stronger here.

## 0.7 Results
- The far field at 2.4 GHz is doughnut shaped, and the 1D plot shows a figure of eight, as a dipole should.
- Surface current flows along the arms from the feed.
