> [!abstract] Key idea
> Design equations give a **first estimate** of the patch size from the target frequency. Simulation shows where the antenna really resonates, and **parametric sweeps** tune it onto the target.

## 0.2 Patch Design Equations
- **Inputs:** operating frequency **f0**, the substrate's **relative permittivity εr**, and its **thickness h**. c is the speed of light, 3 × 10⁸ m/s.
- **Outputs:** patch width **W** and patch length **L**.
- **εeff** is lower than εr, because part of the field travels in the air above the substrate.
- **ΔL** covers fringing: fields spill past the patch edges, so the patch acts longer than it is. That's why 2ΔL is subtracted.

### 0.2.1 Worked Example: 13 GHz Patch
Inputs: f0 = 13 GHz, FR-4 with εr = 4.3, h = 1.57 mm.

| Quantity | Result |
|---|---|
| Width W | **7.09 mm** |
| Effective permittivity εeff | **3.51** |
| Length extension ΔL | **0.68 mm** |
| Length L | **4.79 mm** |

> [!warning] Lecture values differ
> The lecture shows W = 7.14 mm, εeff = 3.10 and L = 5.1 mm. Working the equations through gives the values above, so use these.

> [!info] Why equations are only a starting point
> They come from a simplified model that assumes a thin substrate. Here h is about 13% of the wavelength in the substrate, which is thick. Rounded to W = 7 and L = 5, the first simulation dipped at **15.5 GHz**, not 13 GHz.

## 0.3 Building the Model
- The layers are the same as in [[3. Patch Antenna Design|the 2.4 GHz patch]]: copper patch, FR-4 substrate, copper ground plane.
- Starting feed sizes are rules of thumb, not theory: **feed length = W / 2** and **feed width = L / 2**.
- The substrate must hold the patch and feed: **SL = L + 2 × FL**.
- Load **FR-4 (lossy)** from the material library, so substrate loss is included.
- Simulate a band around the target: 10 to 20 GHz first, then 10 to 15 GHz once the dip is close.

## 0.6 VSWR Check
- **VSWR = (1 + |Γ|) / (1 - |Γ|)**, so it comes straight from the reflection coefficient.
- An **axis marker** reads a plot at an exact frequency.
- The final antenna has **VSWR 1.197 at 13 GHz**, which matches its S11 of about **-21 dB**.

| VSWR | Γ | S11 | Power reflected |
|---|---|---|---|

## 0.7 Efficiency
- **Radiation efficiency** is the share of **accepted** power that radiates. It only counts losses in the materials.
- **Total efficiency** is the share of **input** power that radiates, so it counts mismatch too.
- CST can show both in dB or as a linear value.
