> [!abstract] Key idea
> A **printed quasi-Yagi** swaps metal rods for printed monopoles along the edge of a ground plane, and the ground removes the need for a balun. But a Yagi exists for its **directional gain**, and the lecture judges this one almost entirely by S11, which can't show that.

- The substrate is **FR-4**, 1.6 mm thick, with εr = 4.3 and a loss tangent of 0.025. It's designed for **1.85 GHz** and simulated from 1.4 to 2.8 GHz.
- The **front** has a microstrip line running along the ground's top edge, ending in the driver, which is a printed monopole.
- The **back** has the ground, with the reflector and director growing from its top edge as parasitic monopoles.
- The lengths keep the usual Yagi order: reflector 36.4 mm, driver 30.4 mm, director 15.4 mm.

> [!warning] The feed width doesn't match the table
> The parameter table gives Wf = **3.2 mm**, which works out to about 49 Ω. The lecture enters 2.2 mm, which is about 61 Ω. Use 3.2 mm for a 50 Ω line.

- A dipole driven element is balanced, so feeding it from an unbalanced line normally needs a **balun**.
- A monopole doesn't: the strip feeds the driver, and the ground carries the opposite return current.
- The ground edge acts roughly like a mirror, so each monopole and its image behave like a dipole.
- That makes the beam run **along the ground edge**, from the reflector towards the director.

> [!warning] A fixed marker hides the shift
> The lecture says the frequency doesn't move, but all these values come from one marker at 1.9107 GHz. On the plot, the dips actually move from about **1.93 GHz at 14 mm** to about **1.85 GHz at 18 mm**. 15 mm looks best partly because its dip lands exactly on the marker.

> [!tip] Judge a director by gain
> A director's job is gain. -32 dB and -49 dB both reflect under 0.1% of the power, so choosing 15 mm for its depth gains nothing real. Pick the length from a **gain sweep**, which the lecture doesn't run.

### 0.4.2 Ground Length
- Sweeping Lg from 38 to 45 mm moves the whole row of elements, because they're all defined from the ground edge.
- A **longer ground** moves the dip up and narrows the band.
- A **shorter ground** moves the dip down and widens the band.
- At Lg = 38 mm, the dip is -38.93 dB at 1.90 GHz.
