- The target is **2.45 GHz**, simulated from 1.5 to 3.5 GHz.
- The layers are a copper ground plane, FR-4 (lossy) and a copper patch.
- WF = 3.13 mm works out to **50 Ω** on this substrate.

## 0.2 Resonant Frequency
- The usual first mode of a circular patch has **f ≈ 1.841 c / (2π a √εr)**.
- **a** is the effective radius. It's a little larger than R, because of fringing.

| Quantity | Value |
|---|---|
| Effective radius for R = 17.4 mm | 17.9 mm |
| Formula result for a plain disc | 2.36 GHz |
| Radius the formula suggests for 2.45 GHz | 16.8 mm |
| Simulated dip, with the inset slot | **2.45 GHz** |

- The formula lands within about 4%. The inset slot isn't in the formula, so tune the final value with a sweep.

## 0.3 Building the Patch
- The patch is a **cylinder** with outer radius R. To check it, pick the circle's center, then pick a point on the circle.
- The cut slot is a brick from -R to -R + G, with G = 10 mm, subtracted from the patch.
- Then join the patch and feed line with **Add**. The lecture only does this after adding the port, but it's better done first.

> [!info] Why the slot measures 9.73 mm, not 10 mm
> The patch edge curves away, so the slot's side edge starts 0.27 mm above the bottom of the circle. That leaves 10 - 0.27 = 9.73 mm, so the table's CL is just this measured edge.

> [!warning] The patch isn't centered on the substrate
> LF = 24.75 mm has to run from the substrate edge to the end of the slot, but with everything centered that distance is only 19.875 mm. So the substrate and ground are moved down by **4.875 mm**, which leaves 5 mm above the patch and 14.75 mm below it.

## 0.4 Waveguide Port by Hand
- The macro from [[12. Patch Antenna Without Insets|Section 12]] builds the port automatically, but it can also be sized by hand.
- This lecture extends it by **3 × WF** to each side and **4 × D** above the line, and takes it down to the ground plane.
- These are rules of thumb. The port just has to be big enough to hold the line's fields.
