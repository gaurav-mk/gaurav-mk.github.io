> [!abstract] Key idea
> A circular loop behaves almost exactly like the square one in [[27. Rectangular Loop Antenna|Section 27]]: a one-wavelength resonance, a beam along the axis, and a match that stops near -7 dB. **The radius sets the frequency**, and the loop's impedance sets the depth.

- It's a PEC ring built like the square loop, with the Wire template from 2 to 4 GHz and a 50 Ω discrete port.
- Setting the circumference to one wavelength, C = 2πr, gives r = 100 / 2π = **15.92 mm**.
- That value is the inner radius. A 1 mm ring around it gives the outer radius, R = 16.92 mm.
- The ring is a smaller cylinder subtracted from a larger one, and a brick then cuts the 1 mm port gap.

> [!tip] Use a taller cutter on curved parts
> The ring curves across the width of the cut, so a brick exactly as tall as the ring leaves a thin sliver of metal. The lecture doubles its height to cut through cleanly.

> [!info] The same match limit
