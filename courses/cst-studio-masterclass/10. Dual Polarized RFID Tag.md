> [!abstract] Key idea
> This tag uses **straight slots** etched in a copper patch. A slot only responds to a wave polarized across it, so the vertical and horizontal slots form **two separate channels**. That makes it truly dual polarized.

- A copper patch covers the substrate, and the slots are etched out of it.
- Each of the four sides has **six slots** of different lengths.
- The substrate is **Taconic TLX-8 (lossy)**, simulated from 4 to 14 GHz.

> [!warning] Slot width
> The transcript gives 0.51 mm, but the model is built and measured with **0.21 mm**, so the notes use that.

## 0.2 Building the Slots
- Each slot is a brick. Draw them all, then Subtract them from the patch in one step.
- The vertical slots on one side set the pattern, and **Mirror Copy** makes the other side.
- For the horizontal slots, swap X and Y.

| Slot n | Inner edge at | Outer edge at |
|---|---|---|
| **Formula** | L/2 - G - nt - (n - 1)S | Inner edge + t |
| **Slot 1** | L/2 - G - t | L/2 - G |
| **Slot 2** | L/2 - G - 2t - S | L/2 - G - t - S |

## 0.3 Simulation
- The setup matches the other tags: **Monostatic RCS**, a field probe at Z = 50, and accuracy at -80 dB.
- The RCS plot shows **five notches** for six slots.

> [!info] Why five notches
> The lecture calls one slot a **dummy**, so it adds no notch of its own. A rough half-wavelength estimate also puts the shortest slot near 15 GHz, just above the 14 GHz limit. Widening the band would show whether that notch exists.

## 0.4 Slot Length Encoding
- The tags in Sections 8 and 9 use **presence**: a resonator is there or it isn't.
- This tag uses **position**: changing a slot's length moves its notch to a set frequency.
- That lets **each notch carry two bits**, so five notches give 10 bits.

> Each polarization only sees its own slots, so the two sets can hold **different codes**. That gives 10 bits per polarization, or 20 in total. The symmetric rings in [[8. Chipless RFID Fundamentals|Section 8]] can't do this.
