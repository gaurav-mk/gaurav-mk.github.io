> [!abstract] Key idea
> This is the classic **quasi-Yagi** layout: a printed dipole with directors in front, and the **edge of the ground plane as the reflector**. A microstrip-to-slot feed drives the dipole, so the antenna stays small and covers 5 to 6 GHz with room to spare.

- The substrate is **Taconic TRF-45**, with εr = 4.5 and a very low loss tangent of 0.0035.
- It targets **5 to 6 GHz**, and is simulated from 4 to 10 GHz with field monitors at 5, 5.5 and 6 GHz.
- The front holds the ground, the dipole and two directors. The back holds only the feed.
- There's **no separate reflector**: the ground's top edge does that job.
- The two directors are there to add gain and bandwidth.
- The board is only about 0.6 λ square at 5.5 GHz.

> [!warning] Use 0.61 mm, not 0.16 mm
> The parameter table lists a substrate thickness of 0.16 mm, but the lecture changes it to **0.61 mm** just before adding the port, so the results below are for 0.61 mm. The table value looks like swapped digits.

- This is the same **microstrip-to-slot** idea as [[22. Semi Elliptical Wideband Folded Dipole|Section 22]], and here the slot really is cut through the ground. The diagrams simplify the stub's shape.
- The thin line on the back crosses the slot, and the radial stub makes the crossing act like a wideband short.
- The signal then travels up the slot and along the **two arms**, which form a two-strip line that drives the dipole.
- On 0.61 mm, the 2 mm line works out to about 35 Ω, and the thin line is far higher. So the steps in width are part of the matching, not a plain 50 Ω line.

- S11 stays below -10 dB from about **4.7 to 6.7 GHz**, a bandwidth of about 35%.
- That covers the 5 to 6 GHz target and the **5 GHz WLAN band**.

| Frequency | Directivity | Radiation efficiency | Total efficiency |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5 GHz | 5.27 dBi | 96.7% | 92.7% |
| 5.5 GHz | 5.46 dBi | 95.9% | 94.8% |
| 6 GHz | 5.77 dBi | Not shown | Not shown |

- Directivity stays steady across the band, so the wide S11 band is matched by a usable pattern.
- The efficiency of about **96%** shows the benefit of a low-loss laminate. FR-4 in [[24. Printed Quasi Yagi Antenna|Section 24]] reached only 80%.

> [!warning] Directional, but not highly directional
> The lecture calls this highly directional, but at 6 GHz it has 5.77 dBi and a 3 dB beamwidth of **151°** in the Phi = 90 cut. That's only about 3.6 dB above a dipole. It's normal for a compact quasi-Yagi, which trades peak gain for small size and wide bandwidth.
