- It's a **rectangular printed monopole**, built with the **Planar** template and simulated from 1 to 5 GHz.
- The target band is 1.7 to 4 GHz.
- The **partial ground** only covers the feed line's side of the back face, as in [[15. Monopole Antenna|Section 15]].
- The substrate is glass epoxy (FR-4), 1.6 mm thick, with **εr = 4.2**.
- The 3 mm feed line works out to about 52 Ω on this substrate.

> [!tip] Editing a material
> The library's FR-4 has εr = 4.3. To use 4.2, open it under **Materials** in the navigation tree and change the value. Only this project is affected.

## 0.2 Placing the Patch
- The patch starts centered, so the 27.7 mm feed line runs into it.
- It's moved up by **D = 5.9 mm**, so its bottom edge meets the top of the feed line.
- Join the patch and feed line with Add, then build the waveguide port from the picked face.

> [!info] The gap that matters
> The ground stops 2.7 mm below the patch (LF - LG). This small gap has a strong effect on matching, because the patch isn't sitting over any ground.

- S11 stays below -10 dB from **1.86 to 3.97 GHz**, which is 2.11 GHz wide.
- That falls a little short of the 1.7 GHz target at the low end.
- The far field at 3 GHz is **doughnut shaped**, so it's nearly omnidirectional.
- Surface current flows from the waveguide port up the feed line and patch.

> [!tip] Estimating the first resonance
> A common estimate for planar monopoles is **f ≈ 7.2 / ((L + r + p) × k)** in GHz, with lengths in cm. Here r = W / 2π, p is the gap, and k ≈ 1.15 for FR-4. It gives 2.29 GHz, which matches the deep dip near 2.3 GHz.
