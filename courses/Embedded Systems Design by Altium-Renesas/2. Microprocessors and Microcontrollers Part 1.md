> [!abstract] Key idea
> A **microprocessor (MPU)** is just the CPU and needs external memory and I/O. It suits heavy processing under a full OS. A **microcontroller (MCU)** puts the CPU, memory and peripherals on **one chip**, which suits low-cost, low-power control. Choosing a chip means **matching its specifications to the application's needs**.

- A **microprocessor** is an IC that acts as the **CPU** of a computer. It's **programmable, clock-driven and register-based**.
- It takes binary input, processes it according to instructions, and produces output.
- It has three key units:
  - the **ALU (arithmetic logic unit)**, which does the math and logic operations
  - the **control unit**, which manages the flow of instructions and data
  - the **register array**, the fastest temporary storage, such as registers A, B, C and D.
- It **needs external memory and I/O chips** to work.

### 0.1.2 The Microcontroller
- A **microcontroller** is a small computer on **a single chip**, built for specific tasks in embedded systems.
- Besides the CPU, it holds **RAM and flash/ROM memory** and programmable **I/O peripherals** such as timers, ADCs and serial interfaces.
- It's **self-contained**: add sensors for input and actuators for output, and it works.
- The CPU **fetches and executes instructions from memory** in an endless input, process, output loop.

### 0.1.3 Comparison
| Feature | Microcontroller (MCU) | Microprocessor (MPU) |
|---|---|---|
| **Integration** | All in one: CPU, memory, I/O, clock and power management | CPU only, with external memory, I/O controllers, clock and power circuits |
| **Processing power** | Moderate, ideal for **control** tasks | High, for heavy and complex **data processing** |
| **Power** | Low, optimized for batteries | High, often needs cooling |
| **Cost and complexity** | Low cost, compact, simple | Higher, because of the extra chips |
| **Security** | Lightweight, integrated | Advanced and scalable |
| **Real-time** | Excellent, bare-metal or RTOS | Runs a full OS (Linux, Windows). Real-time parts run on an RTOS beside it, often under a hypervisor |
| **Uses** | Appliances, smart devices, sensors, car systems | PCs, tablets, servers, multimedia, edge computing |

### 0.2.1 CPU Core and Instruction Set
- An MCU has **one or more cores**. Common architectures are **Arm Cortex-M, AVR, PIC and RISC-V**, plus Renesas' own RL78 and RX.
- The architecture sets the processing capability and the features available.
- **RISC (reduced instruction set computing)** uses simple instructions that run fast, and is common in MCUs. **CISC (complex instruction set computing)** packs more work into each instruction. Renesas RL78 and RX are CISC cores.
- **Bit width** is how much data one instruction handles: **8-bit** is simple and cheap, while **32-bit** is powerful and suits complex or fast tasks.
- **Harvard** architecture has separate buses for instructions and data, so it can fetch both at once. **Von Neumann** shares one bus for both.

### 0.2.2 Memory
- **Flash** is non-volatile and stores the code and firmware. It's typically **32 KB to 2 MB**.
- **RAM**, mostly **SRAM**, holds variables and buffers while the program runs. It's typically **2 KB to 512 KB**.
- Many MCUs also have **data flash or EEPROM** to keep settings across power cycles.
- More memory allows more complex applications, but **it costs more**.

### 0.2.3 Clock Speed
- The clock sets the **instruction execution rate**, measured in **MHz**.
- **Higher clock speed means faster computing, but more power**. Dynamic power rises roughly in proportion to clock frequency.
- Renesas examples: RL78 at 32 MHz, RA6M3 at 120 MHz, RH850 at 400 MHz, RA8M1 at 480 MHz.
- Clock speed isn't the whole story. The work done per cycle depends on the architecture, so compare benchmarks such as CoreMark, not just MHz.

- An **interrupt** is a hardware feature that **pauses the running code** so the MCU can **react instantly** to an internal or external event.
- The **number of interrupts and their priority levels** decide how good the real-time response is.
- Interrupts are essential in **timing-critical and safety-related** applications.
- They also save power: the CPU can sleep until an interrupt wakes it, instead of constantly checking (**polling**).

### 0.2.5 Input/Output Peripherals
- **GPIO (general-purpose input/output) pins** connect to sensors, displays and actuators.
- **Serial communication** uses UART, SPI and I2C.
- The **ADC** reads analog signals, and **PWM (pulse width modulation)** generates pulses, for example to set a motor's speed or an LED's brightness.
- Advanced connectivity includes **CAN, USB and Ethernet**.

### 0.2.6 Timers and Counters
- Built-in timers give **precise timing**.
- They generate accurate **pulses and waveforms**, including PWM.
- They also work as **counters** of internal or external events.
- Uses include communication protocols, **signal measurement** and PWM control.

### 0.2.7 Power and Packaging
- MCUs typically run at **1.8 V to 5 V**, and power efficiency varies a lot between models.
- Packages include **DIP, QFP, QFN, BGA and wafer-level** packages:
  - **DIP** has through-hole pins and is easy to prototype with.
  - **QFP** has leads on four sides.
  - **QFN** has pads underneath and no leads.
  - **BGA** has solder balls underneath.
  - **Wafer-level** packages are barely bigger than the silicon.
- The package and the power needs **shape the whole board design** and how portable the product can be.

## 0.3 Choosing a Microcontroller
The course lists **nine considerations**:
1. **Project needs:** what the MCU will do (control motors, read sensors, communicate) and where it will be used, for example outdoors in heat or cold.
2. **Computational capability:** how fast it processes instructions, mainly its clock speed. High-accuracy uses such as a **battery management system (BMS)** or **motor control** need more.
3. **Memory size:** enough flash and RAM for the program and its data. **More memory costs more**.
4. **Type and size:** 8-bit, 16-bit or 32-bit.
5. **Power:** a battery device needs a **low-power MCU that sleeps** when idle. A plugged-in device is less critical, but efficiency still helps.
6. **I/O and communication:** list **every peripheral and interface needed**, such as UART, SPI, I2C, USB, Ethernet, ADC and PWM.
7. **Extra features:** built-in timers, security, a real-time clock, or **hardware accelerators**. For example, the RH850/C1M-Ax has an Enhanced Motor Control Unit.
8. **Cost and availability:** within budget, easy to buy and **unlikely to go out of stock**.
9. **Tools and support:** good programming tools, libraries, tutorials and community help make coding and debugging easier.

| Criterion | What to consider | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| **Application** | Task complexity, environment | Sets the performance and features needed |
| **Architecture and bit size** | 8, 16 or 32-bit; Harvard or Von Neumann | Speed, power and complexity |
| **Power** | Battery or mains, sleep modes | Battery life and efficiency |
| **Interfaces** | Communication and I/O peripherals | Compatibility with other devices |
| **Memory** | Flash and RAM size | Room for code and data |
| **Cost and availability** | Price, supply chain stability | Budget and production |
| **Development support** | SDKs, libraries, debug tools, community | Development time and effort |
| **Special features** | Security, timers, real-time clock | Extra functions, future-proofing |

### 0.3.1 Example: Renesas RA6E2
A real datasheet overview shows how these specifications appear in practice.

| Area | RA6E2 features |
|---|---|
| **Core** | 200 MHz 32-bit **Arm Cortex-M33** with NVIC (interrupt controller), SWD (debug port) and ETB (trace buffer) |
| **Memory** | 128 or 256 KB code flash, 4 KB data flash, 32 KB SRAM with parity, 8 KB SRAM with ECC, 1 KB standby SRAM |
| **Analog** | 12-bit ADC (12 channels), 12-bit DAC (2 channels), temperature sensor |
| **Timers** | GPT16E general PWM timers (6 channels), low-power GPT (2 channels), watchdog timer, RTC with calendar |
| **Communication** | USB 2.0 full-speed device (not on the 32-pin package), CAN FD, I3C, SCI ×2, SPI ×2, SSI (audio), QSPI, HDMI CEC |
| **System** | DMA (8 channels), DTC, ELC (event link controller), on-chip oscillators, low-power modes, TrustZone |
| **Safety** | Memory protection unit, SRAM parity and ECC, CRC calculator, independent watchdog (IWDT), data operation circuit, flash area protection, ADC self-test, clock frequency accuracy measurement |
| **Security** | TrustZone, true random number generator (TRNG), 128-bit unique ID |
| **Packages** | QFN 32 and 48, BGA 36 and 64, LQFP 64 |

- **DMA** moves data between peripherals and memory **without the CPU**, which speeds things up. The **DTC** is a lighter data mover for small, interrupt-driven transfers.
- The on-chip oscillators are **HOCO** (16, 18 or 20 MHz), **MOCO** (8 MHz), **LOCO** (32 kHz) and **ILOCO** (15 kHz). Fast clocks give performance and slow ones save power.
- **ADC resolution** is the number of distinct values the ADC can output. A 12-bit ADC gives **4096 levels**, so with a 3.3 V reference each step is about **0.8 mV**.
- **Parity** detects a flipped memory bit, while **ECC** (error-correcting code) also fixes it.
- **TrustZone** splits the chip into **secure and non-secure** worlds, so trusted code is protected from the rest.

## 0.4 MCU Families
- An **MCU family** is a group of MCUs that share the same basic design but differ in features, memory size or speed.
- Within a family you can **move up or down without rewriting your code** from scratch.

| Application | Example RL78 devices | What makes them special |
|---|---|---|
| **General purpose** | RL78/G13, RL78/G22 | Basic features for simple control tasks |
| **Motor control** | RL78/G14, RL78/G1F | Extra features for driving motors efficiently |
| **LCD display** | RL78/L12, RL78/L13 | Built-in LCD driver |
| **Communications** | RL78/G1C, RL78/G1H | USB, Bluetooth or other wireless modules |
| **Automotive** | RL78/F13, RL78/F14 | Car networks (**CAN, LIN**), high temperature |
| **Electricity meter** | RL78/I1B, RL78/I1C | High-precision ADCs for energy measurement |

- **RL78** is Renesas' low-power 8- and 16-bit family. **RA** is the 32-bit Arm Cortex-M family.

- **MCU:** the course picks the **Renesas RL78/G12**, with Arduino as a prototyping option. The Renesas reference design uses the **RL78/G15**. The reasons:
  - **ultra-low power** for long battery life
  - **built-in timers, ADC and GPIO**, which cut external parts
  - low cost and good tools
  - enough I/O pins for the motor, LEDs and switch, in a small package.
- **DC motor:** runs at **3 V** to match the batteries, draws **little current**, gives **enough force** to press the spray, and is small.
- **NPN transistor:** an MCU pin can't supply motor current, so the transistor **switches the motor's larger current using the pin's small current**. Choose it by:
  - **high current gain (hFE)**, so a small base current switches the motor
  - **collector-emitter breakdown voltage (Vceo)** above the motor voltage
  - **collector current rating (Ic)** above the motor's peak current
  - **low saturation voltage (Vce(sat))** for less power loss and heat.

> [!warning] Add a flyback diode
> A motor is an inductor. When the transistor switches it off, it creates a voltage spike that can destroy the transistor. A **diode across the motor** gives that current a safe path.

> [!tip] Why the battery lasts
> The MCU spends almost all its time **asleep at about 1 µA**, and a timer interrupt wakes it to spray. The motor's 550 mA dominates the energy use. If each spray ran it for 1 s every 9 minutes, its average draw would be about 550 mA × 1/540 ≈ **1 mA**, still around a thousand times the MCU's sleep current.

- **Building blocks:**
  - the **image sensor** is the scanner's eye, turning the optical barcode into high-resolution digital data
  - the **MPU** analyzes and decodes the image
  - **memory** includes RAM for fast image processing and storage for the OS and software
  - **interfaces** connect it to users and machines.
- **Why an MPU, not an MCU:** image processing needs **high-speed, multitasking computation**, **large memory** for image data, a **complex OS**, fast interfaces and real-time analytics.
- **Communication:**
  - **USB** is plug-and-play for PCs and point-of-sale systems.
  - **Ethernet** gives reliable wired networking for fixed-mount, high-volume scanners.
  - **Bluetooth and Wi-Fi** let the scanner work untethered.
- **Power management IC (PMIC):** an MPU needs **several voltage rails switched on in a precise order**. A PMIC puts voltage regulation (step-up and step-down converters), **power sequencing**, battery charging and protection, and current and thermal limits into one chip.

#### 0.5.2.1 Renesas RZ/A2M
- An image-processing MPU built for **embedded AI (e-AI)** and **HMI** (human-machine interface) applications.
- It has an **Arm Cortex-A9 at up to 528 MHz**, **up to 4 MB of on-chip SRAM**, camera inputs, high-resolution display support and graphics, 15 PWM outputs with 3-phase motor PWM, and security features.
- It runs at 3 V to 3.6 V and comes in 176 to 324-pin BGA packages.
- Its 4 MB of on-chip RAM lets it run an **RTOS without external DRAM**, which is unusual for an MPU.

#### 0.5.2.2 The DRP (Dynamically Reconfigurable Processor)
- The **DRP** is hardware that **rewires itself for the current task**, switching its function from one moment to the next.
- It shares its circuits both **over time and across the chip** (time and space multiplexing). For vision and AI work, that gives **higher performance and lower power than a CPU, GPU or DSP**.
- It can **accelerate several algorithms in one application**, and Renesas supplies ready-made DRP library elements.
- It suits robots and cameras that must **react instantly without the cloud**.

### 0.5.4 Solid-State Automotive Power Distribution Module (RL78)
- It replaces a car's fuses and relays with **intelligent power devices (IPDs)**. These are smart switches with built-in protection and current sensing.
- The MCU needs:
  - **CAN and LIN** to talk to the other ECUs in the car. **LIN** is a cheap single-wire bus for simple nodes.
  - **plenty of I/O pins** to drive the power switches and read their status
  - an **ADC** to measure current and voltage, so it can catch **overloads and short circuits**
  - **low-power modes**, because the module is always on and mustn't drain the car battery.
- The automotive **RL78/F13 and F14**, with CAN, LIN and high-temperature support, fit this job.
